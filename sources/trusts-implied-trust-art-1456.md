# Source text
Art. 1441. Trusts are either express or implied. Express trusts are created by the intention of the trustor or of the parties. Implied trusts come into being by operation of law.

Art. 1456. If property is acquired through mistake or fraud, the person obtaining it is, by force of law, considered a trustee of an implied trust for the benefit of the person from whom the property comes.

Art. 1144. The following actions must be brought within ten years from the time the right of action accrues: (1) Upon a written contract; (2) Upon an obligation created by law; (3) Upon a judgment.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- Express trusts arise from the parties' intention; implied trusts arise by operation of law (Art. 1441).
- A person who acquires property through mistake or fraud holds it as trustee of an implied trust for the person from whom it came (Art. 1456).
- An implied trust is an obligation created by law, so an action for reconveyance generally prescribes in ten years (Art. 1144), counted from registration of the property in the trustee's name, which is treated as a repudiation of the trust.
- Exception (settled jurisprudence): if the person seeking reconveyance is in actual, continuous possession of the property, prescription does not run, because the action is in the nature of a suit to quiet title.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Through a clerical mistake at the Registry, Dino's lot was titled in the name of his neighbour Eli in 2012. Dino moved to Cebu in 2012 and left the lot vacant. Eli has occupied it since. In 2025 Dino sues Eli for reconveyance. Has the action prescribed?
Model answer: Yes. Eli acquired title through mistake, so he holds the lot under an implied trust for Dino (Art. 1456). The action for reconveyance is on an obligation created by law and prescribes in ten years (Art. 1144), counted from the 2012 registration. Dino was not in possession, so the exception does not apply, and his 2025 suit is late.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. A similar issue was asked in the 2024 Bar Examination, Civil Law.) Siblings Lorna and Nestor inherited a house and lot from their mother in 2010. Nestor has lived in the house continuously since then. In 2011, Lorna executed an affidavit falsely stating that she was the sole heir, and a title was issued in her name alone. In 2025, Lorna sent Nestor a letter asking him to leave. Nestor then sued Lorna for reconveyance of his one-half share. Lorna argues that the action is barred because more than ten years have passed since the title was issued in 2011. Is Lorna correct? Explain.
Model answer:
(Answer) No. Nestor's action has not prescribed.

(Legal basis) Under Art. 1456, a person who acquires property through mistake or fraud holds it as trustee of an implied trust for the person from whom it came. An action for reconveyance on an implied trust generally prescribes in ten years from registration of the title (Art. 1144). But when the plaintiff is in actual, continuous possession of the property, prescription does not run, because the action is in the nature of a suit to quiet title.

(Application) By falsely claiming to be the sole heir, Lorna obtained title to Nestor's half and holds it in implied trust for him. The ten years would ordinarily run from the 2011 title. However, Nestor has lived on the property continuously since 2010, so prescription never began to run against him.

(Conclusion) Because Nestor is in possession, his action is imprescriptible, and Lorna's defence of prescription fails.
