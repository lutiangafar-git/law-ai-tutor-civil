# Source text
Art. 2047. By guaranty a person, called the guarantor, binds himself to the creditor to fulfill the obligation of the principal debtor in case the latter should fail to do so. If a person binds himself solidarily with the principal debtor, the provisions of Section 4, Chapter 3, Title I of this Book shall be observed. In such case the contract is called a suretyship.

Art. 2058. The guarantor cannot be compelled to pay the creditor unless the latter has exhausted all the property of the debtor, and has resorted to all the legal remedies against the debtor.

Art. 2059. The excussion shall not take place: (1) If the guarantor has expressly renounced it; (2) If he has bound himself solidarily with the debtor; (3) In case of insolvency of the debtor; (4) When he has absconded, or cannot be sued within the Philippines unless he has left a manager or representative; (5) If it may be presumed that an execution on the property of the principal debtor would not result in the satisfaction of the obligation.

Art. 1216. The creditor may proceed against any one of the solidary debtors or some or all of them simultaneously. The demand made against one of them shall not be an obstacle to those which may subsequently be directed against the others, so long as the debt has not been fully collected.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- A guarantor pays only if the principal debtor fails to pay (Art. 2047).
- A person who binds himself solidarily with the principal debtor is a surety, and the rules on solidary obligations apply (Art. 2047).
- A guarantor has the benefit of excussion: the creditor must first exhaust the debtor's property and legal remedies (Art. 2058).
- There is no excussion when the guarantor has bound himself solidarily with the debtor (Art. 2059(2)).
- The creditor may proceed against any solidary debtor, including a surety, without first suing the principal debtor (Art. 1216).
- What the parties call the document does not control; its terms decide whether it is a guaranty or a suretyship.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Carlo borrowed P500,000 from Dela Bank. His uncle Lito signed a separate document promising to pay the bank "if Carlo cannot pay after the bank has gone after Carlo's property." Carlo defaults. Can the bank sue Lito at once?
Model answer: No. Lito did not bind himself solidarily; he promised to pay only if Carlo fails to pay, so he is a guarantor under Art. 2047. Under Art. 2058 he has the benefit of excussion: the bank must first exhaust Carlo's property and legal remedies before it can compel Lito to pay.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. A similar issue was asked in the 2017 Bar Examination, Civil Law.) Bayani Lending Corp. lent P2 Million to Tess, payable in one year. Tess's brother Ramon signed a one-page document titled "Guaranty Undertaking" in which he "binds himself jointly and severally with Tess for the full payment of the loan." When Tess failed to pay on maturity, Bayani Lending sued Ramon alone, without first going after Tess. Ramon moved to dismiss, arguing that as a guarantor he has the benefit of excussion, as the title of the document shows. Should the motion be granted? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: No. Ramon is a surety, so Bayani Lending may sue him without first proceeding against Tess.
- Legal basis: Under Art. 2047, a person who binds himself solidarily with the principal debtor is a surety, and the rules on solidary obligations apply. The creditor may proceed against any solidary debtor (Art. 1216). The benefit of excussion (Art. 2058) is not available when the guarantor has bound himself solidarily with the debtor (Art. 2059(2)).
- Application: The document's title says "guaranty", but its terms control: Ramon bound himself "jointly and severally", that is, solidarily, with Tess. His undertaking is a suretyship. As a surety he has no benefit of excussion, so Bayani Lending may go after him directly.
- Conclusion: The motion to dismiss should be denied; Ramon is liable as a surety upon Tess's default.

# Objective and multiple-choice quiz (Year 1-2)
Q (multiple choice): A person who binds himself SOLIDARILY with the principal debtor is a:
A) guarantor
B) surety
C) pledgee
D) depositary
Correct: B
Hint: Look at the second sentence of Art. 2047.
Hint: The contract in that case "is called a suretyship".
Why: Under Art. 2047, binding oneself solidarily with the principal debtor makes the contract a suretyship.

Q (multiple choice): A guarantor who has NOT bound himself solidarily may ask the creditor to first:
A) sue the guarantor's spouse
B) exhaust all the property of the debtor and all legal remedies against him
C) wait five years
D) get a court order to sell the guarantor's house
Correct: B
Hint: This is the benefit described in Art. 2058.
Hint: The creditor must go after the debtor first.
Why: Under Art. 2058, the guarantor cannot be compelled to pay unless the creditor has exhausted the debtor's property and legal remedies.

Q (objective): What is the name of the guarantor's right under Art. 2058 to require the creditor to go after the debtor first?
Accept: benefit of excussion | excussion | benefit of exhaustion
Hint: Art. 2059 begins "The ______ shall not take place".
Why: Art. 2058 gives the guarantor the benefit of excussion; Art. 2059 lists when it does not apply.

Q (objective): Under Art. 1216, the creditor may proceed against any one of the ______ debtors, or all of them at once. (One word.)
Accept: solidary
Hint: It is the kind of liability a surety has.
Why: Art. 1216 lets the creditor proceed against any one of the solidary debtors or all of them simultaneously.
