# Source text
Art. 440. The ownership of property gives the right by accession to everything which is produced thereby, or which is incorporated or attached thereto, either naturally or artificially.

Art. 457. To the owners of lands adjoining the banks of rivers belong the accretion which they gradually receive from the effects of the current of the waters.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- Ownership gives the right by accession to everything produced by the property or incorporated or attached to it, naturally or artificially (Art. 440).
- Alluvion: owners of land adjoining river banks own the accretion they gradually receive from the effects of the current of the waters (Art. 457).
- Requisites: the deposit is gradual; it is caused by the current of the river; and the land where it attaches adjoins the bank of the river.
- A sudden deposit, or one made by human works rather than the current, does not fall under Art. 457.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Over ten years, soil carried by the current of the Pampanga River slowly built up along Nena's riverside lot, adding 300 square metres. Nena's inland neighbour Oca says the new strip should be shared because it also touches the boundary of his lot. Who owns the strip?
Model answer: Nena. Under Art. 457, accretion gradually received from the current of a river belongs to the owner of the land adjoining the river bank. Nena's lot is the one on the bank, and the deposit was gradual and caused by the current, so the accretion is hers. Oca's lot does not adjoin the river.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. A similar issue was asked in the 2017 Bar Examination, Civil Law.) Inday owns a titled farm lot along the Cagayan River. Over fifteen years, soil carried by the river's current slowly built up along her riverbank, adding about 500 square metres of dry land. The municipality now fences the new strip, claiming that it is part of the riverbank and therefore belongs to the public. Inday insists the strip is hers. Who owns the strip? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: Inday owns the 500-square-metre strip.
- Legal basis: Under Art. 440, ownership gives the right by accession to everything incorporated or attached to the property naturally. Under Art. 457, owners of land adjoining the banks of rivers own the accretion they gradually receive from the effects of the current of the waters.
- Application: Inday's lot adjoins the bank of the Cagayan River. The strip formed gradually, over fifteen years, from soil deposited by the river's current. All the requisites of alluvion are present: a gradual deposit, caused by the current, on land adjoining the bank. The law gives such accretion to the riparian owner, not to the public.
- Conclusion: As riparian owner, Inday owns the strip by accession under Arts. 440 and 457, and the municipality's claim fails.
