# Source text
Art. 1868. By the contract of agency a person binds himself to render some service or to do something in representation or on behalf of another, with the consent or authority of the latter.

Art. 1920. The principal may revoke the agency at will, and compel the agent to return the document evidencing the agency. Such revocation may be express or implied.

Art. 1927. An agency cannot be revoked if a bilateral contract depends upon it, or if it is the means of fulfilling an obligation already contracted, or if a partner is appointed manager of a partnership in the contract of partnership and his removal from the management is unjustifiable.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- In agency, the agent acts in representation or on behalf of the principal, with the principal's consent or authority (Art. 1868).
- General rule: the principal may revoke the agency at will, expressly or impliedly (Art. 1920).
- Exception: the agency cannot be revoked if a bilateral contract depends on it, or if it is the means of fulfilling an obligation already contracted (Art. 1927).
- An agency that serves the interest of the agent as well as the principal (an agency coupled with an interest) is not revocable at will.
- The agent's commission alone does not make an agency irrevocable; the agent's interest must go beyond the commission.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Rosa authorised her cousin Ben in writing to lease out her apartment and collect the rent. Ben's only compensation was a 5% commission on rent collected. After three months Rosa told Ben she would manage the apartment herself. Can Rosa end Ben's authority?
Model answer: Yes. Under Art. 1920 the principal may revoke the agency at will. Ben's only interest is his commission, which does not bring the agency under the exceptions in Art. 1927, since no bilateral contract depends on the agency and it is not the means of fulfilling an obligation already contracted. Rosa may revoke it and ask Ben to return the written authority.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. A similar issue was asked in the 2015 Bar Examination, Civil Law.) Marites owes her friend Jun, an accountant, P400,000 for bookkeeping services he rendered last year. To settle the debt, Marites signs a Special Power of Attorney authorising Jun to sell her condominium unit for not less than P5 Million, and they agree in writing that Jun will keep P400,000 from the proceeds as payment of the debt, plus a 3% commission. Two weeks later, Marites's cousin offers to buy the unit for P5.5 Million. Marites sends Jun a letter revoking the Special Power of Attorney and asks him to return it. Is the revocation effective? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: No. Marites cannot revoke Jun's authority on her own.
- Legal basis: As a rule, the principal may revoke an agency at will (Art. 1920). But under Art. 1927, an agency cannot be revoked if it is the means of fulfilling an obligation already contracted. An agency created for the benefit of both principal and agent is not revocable at will.
- Application: The sale was agreed as the means of paying the P400,000 Marites already owed Jun. Jun's interest therefore goes beyond his 3% commission: the agency secures payment of an existing debt, so it benefits him as well as Marites. A better offer from her cousin does not change the nature of the agency.
- Conclusion: The agency is coupled with an interest and falls under Art. 1927, so Marites's letter does not validly revoke it.
