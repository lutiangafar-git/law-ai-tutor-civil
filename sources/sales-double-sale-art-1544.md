# Source text
Art. 1458. By the contract of sale one of the contracting parties obligates himself to transfer the ownership and to deliver a determinate thing, and the other to pay therefor a price certain in money or its equivalent. A contract of sale may be absolute or conditional.

Art. 1544. If the same thing should have been sold to different vendees, the ownership shall be transferred to the person who may have first taken possession thereof in good faith, if it should be movable property. Should it be immovable property, the ownership shall belong to the person acquiring it who in good faith first recorded it in the Registry of Property. Should there be no inscription, the ownership shall pertain to the person who in good faith was first in the possession; and, in the absence thereof, to the person who presents the oldest title, provided there is good faith.

Art. 1874. When a sale of a piece of land or any interest therein is through an agent, the authority of the latter shall be in writing; otherwise, the sale shall be void.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- In a sale, the seller obligates himself to transfer ownership and deliver a determinate thing; the buyer pays a price certain in money or its equivalent (Art. 1458).
- Double sale (Art. 1544) applies when the same thing is validly sold by the same seller to different buyers.
- Movables: ownership goes to the buyer who first took possession in good faith (Art. 1544).
- Immovables: first, the buyer who in good faith first recorded the sale in the Registry of Property; if none recorded, the buyer first in possession in good faith; if none, the buyer with the oldest title in good faith (Art. 1544).
- A sale of land through an agent is void unless the agent's authority is in writing (Art. 1874). A void sale gives the buyer no right, so it cannot compete under the double-sale rules.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: On Monday, Mark sold his guitar to Nina, who paid and agreed to pick it up on Friday. On Wednesday, Mark sold the same guitar to Oscar, who did not know of the first sale, and handed it to him that day. Who owns the guitar?
Model answer: Oscar. A guitar is movable property, so under Art. 1544 ownership goes to the buyer who first took possession in good faith. Oscar received the guitar first and did not know of the sale to Nina, so he owns it. Nina's remedy is against Mark.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (2010 Bar Examination, Civil Law, Question XVI) X was the owner of an unregistered parcel of land in Cabanatuan City. As she was abroad, she advised her sister Y via overseas call to sell the land and sign a contract of sale on her behalf. Y thus sold the land to B1 on March 31, 2001 and executed a deed of absolute sale on behalf of X. B1 fully paid the purchase price. B2, unaware of the sale of the land to B1, signified to Y his interest to buy it but asked Y for her authority from X. Without informing X that she had sold the land to B1, Y sought X for a written authority to sell. X e-mailed Y an authority to sell the land. Y thereafter sold the land on May 1, 2001 to B2 on monthly installment basis for two years, the first installment to be paid at the end of May 2001. Who between B1 and B2 has a better right over the land? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: B2 has the better right over the land.
- Legal basis: Under Art. 1874 of the Civil Code, a sale of land through an agent is void unless the agent's authority is in writing. The double-sale rule of Art. 1544 applies only when the same property has been validly sold to different buyers.
- Application: When Y sold the land to B1, her only authority was X's overseas call, which is not in writing, so the sale to B1 is void. Since the first sale is void, there is no double sale and Art. 1544 does not decide the case. When Y sold to B2, she already had X's written authority sent by e-mail, so the sale to B2 is valid. Paying the price in installments does not make the sale invalid.
- Conclusion: B2, as the buyer under the only valid sale, has the better right over the land.

# Objective and multiple-choice quiz (Year 1-2)
Q (multiple choice): In a double sale of a MOVABLE, who becomes the owner under Art. 1544?
A) The buyer who paid first
B) The buyer who first took possession in good faith
C) The buyer with the oldest title
D) The buyer who registered first
Correct: B
Hint: Art. 1544 has one rule for movables and a different order for immovables.
Hint: For movables, the rule is about possession.
Why: Under Art. 1544, ownership of a movable sold twice goes to the buyer who first took possession in good faith.

Q (multiple choice): A sale of land through an agent whose authority is NOT in writing is:
A) valid
B) voidable
C) unenforceable
D) void
Correct: D
Hint: Look at Art. 1874.
Hint: Art. 1874 ends with the words "otherwise, the sale shall be …".
Why: Art. 1874 says that when land is sold through an agent, the agent's authority must be in writing; otherwise, the sale is void.

Q (objective): For immovables, which public office's records decide a double sale first: the buyer who in good faith first recorded the sale in the ______ of Property?
Accept: registry | registry of property
Hint: It is where sales of land are recorded.
Why: Under Art. 1544, for immovables the first buyer to record the sale in good faith in the Registry of Property owns the land.

Q (objective): In every rule of Art. 1544, the buyer who wins must have acted in good ______.
Accept: faith | good faith
Hint: A buyer who knew of the earlier sale cannot win.
Why: Each priority rule in Art. 1544 requires good faith.
