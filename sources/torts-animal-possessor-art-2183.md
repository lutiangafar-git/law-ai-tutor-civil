# Source text
Art. 2176. Whoever by act or omission causes damage to another, there being fault or negligence, is obliged to pay for the damage done. Such fault or negligence, if there is no pre-existing contractual relation between the parties, is called a quasi-delict and is governed by the provisions of this Chapter.

Art. 2183. The possessor of an animal or whoever may make use of the same is responsible for the damage which it may cause, although it may escape or be lost. This responsibility shall cease only in case the damage should come from force majeure or from the fault of the person who has suffered damage.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- A quasi-delict is damage caused by fault or negligence where there is no pre-existing contract between the parties (Art. 2176).
- The possessor or user of an animal is liable for damage it causes, even if it escapes or is lost (Art. 2183). The injured person need not prove the possessor's negligence.
- That liability ends only in two cases: the damage came from force majeure, or from the fault of the person who suffered the damage (Art. 2183).

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Pia's dog slipped out through a gate that her helper forgot to close and bit Quin, a jogger who was simply passing on the street. Pia says she is not liable because she was at work and did nothing wrong. Is she liable?
Model answer: Yes. Under Art. 2183 the possessor of an animal is liable for the damage it causes even if it escapes. Liability ends only for force majeure or the fault of the injured person. An open gate is not force majeure, and Quin did nothing to provoke the dog, so Pia is liable to Quin.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. A similar issue was asked in the 2010 Bar Examination, Civil Law.) Lito keeps his Rottweiler inside a fenced yard with a large "Beware of Dog" sign on the gate. Mara, an adult neighbour, wanted to take a photo of the dog. Despite the sign and Lito's earlier warnings, she reached over the fence to pat its head, and the dog bit her hand. Mara sues Lito for damages, arguing that the possessor of an animal is liable for any damage it causes. Is Lito liable? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: No. Lito is not liable for Mara's injury.
- Legal basis: Under Art. 2183, the possessor of an animal is responsible for the damage it causes, even if it escapes or is lost. That responsibility ceases only when the damage comes from force majeure or from the fault of the person who suffered the damage.
- Application: The dog never left Lito's fenced yard. Mara was bitten only because she reached over the fence to touch it, despite the warning sign and Lito's earlier warnings. Her injury came from her own fault, which is one of the two grounds that end the possessor's liability.
- Conclusion: Because the damage came from Mara's own fault, Lito is not liable under Art. 2183.
