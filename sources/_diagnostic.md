## civ-01
area: Persons and Family Relations
syllabusRef: III.A.1.b
syllabusYear: 2027
askedIn: Original bar-style question. A similar issue was asked in the 2023 Bar Examination (Civil Law).
question: Ana and Ben, both 25 and single, were married by a judge. They had no marriage license and had been sweethearts for only two years. What is the status of their marriage?
answer: The marriage is void from the beginning. A valid marriage license is a formal requisite, and its absence makes the marriage void ab initio. They are not exempt from the license requirement because they had not lived together as husband and wife for at least five years.
keyPoints: A marriage license is a formal requisite (Art. 3) | Absence of a formal requisite makes the marriage void ab initio (Art. 4) | The five-year cohabitation exemption (Art. 34) does not apply
citation: Family Code, Arts. 3, 4 and 34
law: Art. 4. The absence of any of the essential or formal requisites shall render the marriage void ab initio, except as stated in Article 35 (2).

## civ-02
area: Persons and Family Relations
syllabusRef: III.A.4
syllabusYear: 2027
askedIn: Original bar-style question. A similar issue was asked in the 2023 Bar Examination (Civil Law).
question: Carlo's first marriage was void because no marriage license was issued. Without going to court, he married Ella, believing he was free to marry. Is his marriage to Ella valid?
answer: No. The marriage to Ella is void. The absolute nullity of a previous marriage may be invoked for purposes of remarriage only on the basis of a final judgment declaring that previous marriage void. Carlo remarried without such a judgment, so the second marriage is a void bigamous marriage.
keyPoints: A final judgment declaring the prior marriage void is required before remarrying (Art. 40) | Without it, the subsequent marriage is void | Good faith belief does not cure the lack of a judicial declaration
citation: Family Code, Arts. 35(4) and 40
law: Art. 40. The absolute nullity of a previous marriage may be invoked for purposes of remarriage on the basis solely of a final judgment declaring such previous marriage void.

## civ-03
area: Persons and Family Relations
syllabusRef: III.A.4
syllabusYear: 2027
askedIn: Original bar-style question. A similar issue was asked in the 2024 Bar Examination (Civil Law).
question: Dante's wife has been missing for five years and he believes she is dead. He wants to remarry. What must he do first, and why?
answer: He must first obtain a judicial declaration of presumptive death of his absent wife in a summary proceeding. Under Art. 41, a marriage contracted while a prior marriage subsists is void unless the absent spouse had been absent for four consecutive years with a well-founded belief of death, and the present spouse obtains a declaration of presumptive death in a summary proceeding.
keyPoints: Absence of four consecutive years and a well-founded belief that the spouse is dead (Art. 41) | A summary proceeding declaring presumptive death is required for remarriage | Without it, the second marriage is void
citation: Family Code, Art. 41
law: For the purpose of contracting the subsequent marriage under the preceding paragraph the spouse present must institute a summary proceeding as provided in this Code for the declaration of presumptive death of the absentee, without prejudice to the effect of reappearance of the absent spouse.

## civ-04
area: Persons and Family Relations
syllabusRef: II.A.1
syllabusYear: 2027
askedIn: Original bar-style question. A similar issue was asked in the 2022 Bar Examination (Civil Law).
question: A child with an intra-uterine life of six months was completely delivered alive but died 12 hours later. Is the child considered born for civil purposes?
answer: No. For a foetus with an intra-uterine life of less than seven months, the child is not deemed born if it dies within twenty-four hours after its complete delivery from the maternal womb. The child died within 12 hours, so it is not considered born.
keyPoints: Birth determines personality (Art. 40) | Less than seven months of intra-uterine life: must survive 24 hours after complete delivery (Art. 41) | The child died within 24 hours
citation: Civil Code, Arts. 40 and 41
law: However, if the foetus had an intra-uterine life of less than seven months, it is not deemed born if it dies within twenty-four hours after its complete delivery from the maternal womb.

## civ-05
area: Property
syllabusRef: V.C
syllabusYear: 2027
askedIn: Original bar-style question. A similar issue was asked in the 2019 Bar Examination (Civil Law).
question: Eric, in good faith, built a house on Fe's land believing it was his. What are Fe's options as landowner?
answer: Fe may either appropriate the house after paying Eric the indemnity provided by law, or oblige Eric to pay the price of the land. But Eric cannot be obliged to buy the land if its value is considerably more than that of the house; in that case he pays reasonable rent if Fe does not choose to appropriate the house.
keyPoints: Builder in good faith on another's land (Art. 448) | Owner chooses: appropriate the building after paying indemnity, or oblige the builder to pay the price of the land | Builder need not buy the land if it is considerably more valuable; then he pays rent
citation: Civil Code, Art. 448
law: Art. 448. The owner of the land on which anything has been built, sown or planted in good faith, shall have the right to appropriate as his own the works, sowing or planting, after payment of the indemnity provided for in articles 546 and 548, or to oblige the one who built or planted to pay the price of the land, and the one who sowed, the proper rent.

## civ-06
area: Property
syllabusRef: V.E
syllabusYear: 2027
askedIn: Original bar-style question. A similar issue was asked in the 2025 Bar Examination (Civil Law).
question: Gina, Hugo and Ivy co-own a lot in equal shares. Without the others' consent, Gina sold the whole lot to Jun. Is the sale valid?
answer: The sale is valid only as to Gina's one-third ideal share. Each co-owner has full ownership of his part and may alienate it, but the effect of the alienation is limited to the portion that may be allotted to him on the division of the co-ownership. Jun becomes a co-owner with Hugo and Ivy.
keyPoints: Each co-owner may alienate his ideal share (Art. 493) | The sale affects only the seller's share on partition | The buyer steps into the co-ownership; the others' shares are unaffected
citation: Civil Code, Art. 493
law: Art. 493. Each co-owner shall have the full ownership of his part and of the fruits and benefits pertaining thereto, and he may therefore alienate, assign or mortgage it, and even substitute another person in its enjoyment, except when personal rights are involved. But the effect of the alienation or the mortgage, with respect to the co-owners, shall be limited to the portion which may be allotted to him in the division upon the termination of the co-ownership.

## civ-07
area: Property

question: Kiko's lot is surrounded by other lots and has no adequate outlet to a public highway. He did not cause the isolation. Can he demand a right of way through a neighbour's lot, and on what condition?
answer: Yes. The owner of an estate surrounded by other immovables without adequate outlet to a public highway is entitled to demand a right of way through the neighbouring estates, after payment of the proper indemnity. The isolation must not be due to his own acts, and the passage must be at the point least prejudicial to the servient estate.
keyPoints: Estate surrounded by others without adequate outlet to a public highway (Art. 649) | Payment of proper indemnity | Isolation not due to the owner's acts; least prejudicial point (Art. 650)
citation: Civil Code, Arts. 649 and 650
law: Art. 649. The owner, or any person who by virtue of a real right may cultivate or use any immovable, which is surrounded by other immovables pertaining to other persons and without adequate outlet to a public highway, is entitled to demand a right of way through the neighboring estates, after payment of the proper indemnity.

## civ-10
area: Succession
syllabusRef: VII.B.2
syllabusYear: 2027
askedIn: Original bar-style question. A similar issue was asked in the 2024 Bar Examination (Civil Law).
question: In his will, Nico instituted his friend Omar as sole heir and completely omitted his only son, Pao, who was alive when the will was made. What is the effect?
answer: There is preterition of a compulsory heir in the direct line. The institution of Omar as heir is annulled, so Pao inherits by intestacy. Devises and legacies, if any, remain valid insofar as they are not inofficious.
keyPoints: Total omission of a compulsory heir in the direct line is preterition (Art. 854) | Preterition annuls the institution of heir | Devises and legacies stand if not inofficious
citation: Civil Code, Art. 854
law: Art. 854. The preterition or omission of one, some, or all of the compulsory heirs in the direct line, whether living at the time of the execution of the will or born after the death of the testator, shall annul the institution of heir; but the devises and legacies shall be valid insofar as they are not inofficious.

## civ-11
area: Succession

syllabusYear: 2027

question: Rita wrote her will entirely in her own hand and signed it, but did not date it. No witnesses signed. Is it a valid holographic will?
answer: No. A holographic will must be entirely written, dated and signed by the hand of the testator. Witnesses are not required, but the date is. Without a date, the will is not valid as a holographic will.
keyPoints: Holographic will: entirely written, dated and signed by the testator's hand (Art. 810) | No witnesses needed | Missing date: not valid
citation: Civil Code, Art. 810
law: Art. 810. A person may execute a holographic will which must be entirely written, dated, and signed by the hand of the testator himself. It is subject to no other form, and may be made in or out of the Philippines, and need not be witnessed.

## civ-12
area: Succession
syllabusRef: VII.B.4
syllabusYear: 2027
askedIn: Original bar-style question (Civil Law).
question: Sol died leaving three legitimate children and no spouse. In her will she left everything to a charity. What part of the estate is reserved for her children?
answer: One-half of the hereditary estate is the legitime of the legitimate children, divided equally among them. The will's disposition to the charity is valid only as to the free portion (the other half); it must be reduced insofar as it impairs the children's legitime.
keyPoints: Legitime of legitimate children: one-half of the hereditary estate (Art. 888) | The testator may freely dispose only of the free portion | Inofficious dispositions are reduced
citation: Civil Code, Art. 888
law: Art. 888. The legitime of legitimate children and descendants consists of one-half of the hereditary estate of the father and of the mother.

## civ-14
area: Obligations and Contracts
syllabusRef: VIII.A.3.e
syllabusYear: 2027
askedIn: Original bar-style question. A similar issue was asked in the 2025 Bar Examination (Civil Law).
question: Uma and Vic signed a promissory note: 'We promise to pay Lito P100,000.' Nothing else was stated. Can Lito collect the whole P100,000 from Uma alone?
answer: No. The concurrence of two debtors in one obligation does not imply that each must pay the whole; solidarity exists only when expressly stated, or when the law or the nature of the obligation requires it. The obligation is joint, so Lito can collect only P50,000 from Uma.
keyPoints: Solidarity is not presumed (Art. 1207) | Without express stipulation, law or nature of the obligation, the obligation is joint | A joint debt is divided equally (Art. 1208)
citation: Civil Code, Arts. 1207 and 1208
law: Art. 1207. The concurrence of two or more creditors or of two or more debtors in one and the same obligation does not imply that each one of the former has a right to demand, or that each one of the latter is bound to render, entire compliance with the prestation.

## civ-15
area: Obligations and Contracts
syllabusRef: VIII.A.2.b
syllabusYear: 2027
askedIn: Original bar-style question. A similar issue was asked in the 2025 Bar Examination (Civil Law).
question: A lightning-caused fire destroyed the warehouse where Wil kept the rice he had agreed to deliver. He was not late and not negligent. Is he liable to the buyer?
answer: No. Except in cases expressly specified by law, or when declared by stipulation, or when the nature of the obligation requires the assumption of risk, no person is responsible for events that could not be foreseen or were inevitable. The fire was a fortuitous event and Wil was neither in delay nor negligent.
keyPoints: No liability for fortuitous events (Art. 1174) | Exceptions: law, stipulation, or nature of the obligation requires assumption of risk | The debtor must be free from delay and negligence
citation: Civil Code, Art. 1174
law: Art. 1174. Except in cases expressly specified by the law, or when it is otherwise declared by stipulation, or when the nature of the obligation requires the assumption of risk, no person shall be responsible for those events which could not be foreseen, or which, though foreseen, were inevitable.

## civ-16

## civ-17
area: Obligations and Contracts
syllabusRef: VIII.A.5.f

question: Yna owes Zed P200,000 due in June. In May they agreed in writing to move the due date to December. Is the original obligation extinguished by novation?
answer: No. Novation is never presumed. For a new obligation to extinguish the old one, it must be declared in unequivocal terms, or the old and new obligations must be incompatible on every point. Merely extending the period of payment does not extinguish the obligation.
keyPoints: Novation must be declared in unequivocal terms or the obligations must be incompatible on every point (Art. 1292) | Novation is not presumed | A mere change in the period of payment is not novation
citation: Civil Code, Art. 1292
law: Art. 1292. In order that an obligation may be extinguished by another which substitute the same, it is imperative that it be so declared in unequivocal terms, or that the old and the new obligations be on every point incompatible with each other.

law: Art. 1448. There is an implied trust when property is sold, and the legal estate is granted to one party but the price is paid by another for the purpose of having the beneficial interest of the property.

## civ-29

## civ-30
