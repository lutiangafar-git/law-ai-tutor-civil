# Source text
Art. 40. Birth determines personality; but the conceived child shall be considered born for all purposes that are favorable to it, provided it be born later with the conditions specified in the following article.

Art. 41. For civil purposes, the foetus is considered born if it is alive at the time it is completely delivered from the mother's womb. However, if the foetus had an intra-uterine life of less than seven months, it is not deemed born if it dies within twenty-four hours after its complete delivery from the maternal womb.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- Birth determines civil personality (Art. 40).
- A conceived child is considered born for all purposes favorable to it, but only if it is later born under the conditions of Art. 41. This is often called provisional or presumptive personality.
- If the foetus had an intra-uterine life of seven months or more, it is considered born if it is alive at the time of complete delivery from the mother's womb (Art. 41).
- If the foetus had an intra-uterine life of less than seven months, it is not deemed born if it dies within twenty-four hours after complete delivery (Art. 41).
- A donation to a conceived child is a purpose favorable to it, but it takes effect only if the child is later deemed born under Art. 41. If the child is not deemed born, it never had personality and could not be a donee.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: While Lia was still in her mother's womb, her grandfather donated a parcel of land to her, and her mother accepted it for her. Lia was born after eight months of pregnancy, alive, but died two hours after her complete delivery. Was Lia deemed born, and did the donation take effect?
Model answer: Yes. Lia's intra-uterine life was eight months, so under Art. 41 she is considered born because she was alive when completely delivered; the 24-hour rule applies only to an intra-uterine life of less than seven months. Having acquired civil personality, the donation, which was favorable to her as a conceived child under Art. 40, took effect.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (2012 Bar Examination, Civil Law, Question I.b) Ricky donated P1 Million to the unborn child of his pregnant girlfriend, which she accepted. After six (6) months of pregnancy, the fetus was born and baptized as Angela. However, Angela died 20 hours after birth. Ricky sought to recover the P1 Million. Is Ricky entitled to recover? Explain.
Model answer:
(Answer) Yes. Ricky may recover the P1 Million because the donation never took effect.

(Legal basis) Under Art. 40 of the Civil Code, a conceived child is considered born for purposes favorable to it only if it is later born under the conditions of Art. 41. Under Art. 41, a foetus with an intra-uterine life of less than seven months is not deemed born if it dies within twenty-four hours after its complete delivery.

(Application) Angela had an intra-uterine life of only six months and died 20 hours after birth, which is within the 24-hour period. She is therefore not deemed born, never acquired civil personality, and could not become a donee. The provisional personality of a conceived child did not attach because the condition in Art. 41 was not met.

(Conclusion) The donation did not take effect, so the P1 Million must be returned to Ricky.
