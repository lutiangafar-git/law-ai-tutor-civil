# Source text
Art. 854. The preterition or omission of one, some, or all of the compulsory heirs in the direct line, whether living at the time of the execution of the will or born after the death of the testator, shall annul the institution of heir; but the devises and legacies shall be valid insofar as they are not inofficious.

If the omitted compulsory heirs should die before the testator, the institution shall be effectual, without prejudice to the right of representation.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- Preterition is the total omission in the will of a compulsory heir in the direct line, such as a child or descendant, or a parent or ascendant (Art. 854).
- Preterition annuls the institution of heir entirely; devises and legacies stay valid insofar as they are not inofficious (Art. 854). The property covered by the annulled institution passes by intestate succession.
- Only compulsory heirs in the direct line can be preterited. A surviving spouse is a compulsory heir but is not in the direct line, so omitting the spouse is not preterition; the spouse may still claim the legitime.
- If the omitted heir dies before the testator, the institution stays effective, without prejudice to the right of representation (Art. 854).
- In intestate succession, legitimate children exclude the decedent's parents and brothers and sisters; the surviving spouse and illegitimate children inherit together with the legitimate children.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Dan's will gives his entire estate to his close friend Fe. Dan is survived only by his son Gio, who is not mentioned anywhere in the will and received nothing from Dan during his lifetime. Is there preterition, and what happens to the estate?
Model answer: Yes. Gio is a compulsory heir in the direct line who was totally omitted from the will, so there is preterition under Art. 854. The institution of Fe as heir is annulled, and since there are no devises or legacies, the whole estate passes to Gio by intestate succession.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Adapted from the 2024 Bar Examination, Civil Law, Question 10) Caloy died with a net estate of PHP 240 million, survived by his father Cesar, his spouse Grace, his legitimate children Teddy and Bobbie, his illegitimate child Rebreb, and his sisters Alex and Gabbie. In his will, Caloy instituted Rebreb as his sole heir and gave nothing to anyone else. Is the institution of Rebreb as sole heir valid? Who will inherit Caloy's estate? Explain.
Model answer:
(Answer) No. The institution of Rebreb as sole heir is annulled because of preterition, and Caloy's estate passes by intestate succession to Teddy, Bobbie, Grace and Rebreb.

(Legal basis) Under Art. 854 of the Civil Code, the preterition or omission of one, some, or all of the compulsory heirs in the direct line annuls the institution of heir; devises and legacies remain valid insofar as they are not inofficious.

(Application) Teddy and Bobbie are legitimate children, compulsory heirs in the direct line, and the will totally omits them. That is preterition, so the institution of Rebreb is annulled. There are no devises or legacies to preserve, so the whole estate is distributed by intestacy. Grace's omission is not itself preterition because a spouse is not in the direct line, but she inherits in intestacy together with the legitimate children. Rebreb still inherits, but as an illegitimate child in intestacy, not as sole heir. The legitimate children exclude Caloy's father Cesar and his sisters Alex and Gabbie.

(Conclusion) The will's institution of Rebreb is void. The estate goes by intestate succession to Teddy, Bobbie, Grace and Rebreb; Cesar, Alex and Gabbie receive nothing.

# Objective and multiple-choice quiz (Year 1-2)
Q (multiple choice): Under Art. 854, the preterition of a compulsory heir in the direct line:
A) annuls the whole will
B) annuls the institution of heir, but devises and legacies stay valid if not inofficious
C) has no effect if the heir is a child
D) only reduces the legacies
Correct: B
Hint: Art. 854 separates the institution of heir from devises and legacies.
Hint: Devises and legacies are saved "insofar as they are not inofficious".
Why: Art. 854 says preterition annuls the institution of heir; devises and legacies remain valid insofar as they are not inofficious.

Q (multiple choice): Preterition under Art. 854 applies to the omission of compulsory heirs in the:
A) collateral line
B) direct line
C) line of the surviving spouse only
D) line of illegitimate siblings
Correct: B
Hint: Think of children and parents, not brothers and sisters.
Hint: The article says "in the direct line".
Why: Art. 854 covers compulsory heirs in the direct line, whether living when the will was made or born after the testator's death.
