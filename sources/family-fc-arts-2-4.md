# Source text
Art. 2. No marriage shall be valid, unless these essential requisites are present: (1) Legal capacity of the contracting parties who must be a male and a female; and (2) Consent freely given in the presence of the solemnizing officer.

Art. 3. The formal requisites of marriage are: (1) Authority of the solemnizing officer; (2) A valid marriage license except in the cases provided for in Chapter 2 of this Title; and (3) A marriage ceremony which takes place with the appearance of the contracting parties before the solemnizing officer and their personal declaration that they take each other as husband and wife in the presence of not less than two witnesses of legal age.

Art. 4. The absence of any of the essential or formal requisites shall render the marriage void ab initio, except as stated in Article 35 (2). A defect in any of the essential requisites shall render the marriage voidable as provided in Article 45. An irregularity in the formal requisites shall not affect the validity of the marriage but the party or parties responsible for the irregularity shall be civilly, criminally and administratively liable.

Art. 34. No license shall be necessary for the marriage of a man and a woman who have lived together as husband and wife for at least five years and without any legal impediment to marry each other.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- Essential requisites (Art. 2): legal capacity of a male and a female, and consent freely given in the presence of the solemnizing officer.
- Formal requisites (Art. 3): authority of the solemnizing officer, a valid marriage license (unless the marriage is exempt), and a marriage ceremony before the officer with at least two witnesses of legal age.
- Absence of any essential or formal requisite makes the marriage void ab initio, except as stated in Art. 35 (2) (Art. 4).
- A defect in an essential requisite makes the marriage voidable; a mere irregularity in a formal requisite does not affect validity, but the responsible parties are liable (Art. 4).
- Absence of a license is different from an irregularity in it: a license issued by the local civil registrar of a place where neither party resides is an irregularity, so the marriage stays valid.
- A certification by the local civil registrar that, after a diligent search, the marriage license cannot be found in its records can prove that no license was issued (Republic v. Court of Appeals and Castro, G.R. No. 103047, September 2, 1994).
- Some marriages need no license, such as a man and a woman who lived together as husband and wife for at least five years without any legal impediment to marry each other (Art. 34).

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Carlo and Dina, both residents of Quezon City, were married by a judge in the presence of two witnesses of legal age. Their marriage license was issued by the local civil registrar of Pasig City, where neither of them lives. Is their marriage valid?
Model answer: Yes. A marriage license was issued, so the formal requisite is present; getting it from the registrar of a place where neither party resides is only an irregularity in a formal requisite. Under Art. 4, an irregularity does not affect the validity of the marriage, though the persons responsible may be civilly, criminally and administratively liable.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (2016 Bar Examination, Civil Law, Question XIX(a)) Brad and Angelina had a secret marriage before a pastor whose office is located in Arroceros Street, City of Manila. They paid money to the pastor who took care of all the documentation. When Angelina wanted to go to the U.S., she found out that there was no marriage license issued to them before their marriage. Since their marriage was solemnized in 1995 after the effectivity of the Family Code, Angelina filed a petition for judicial declaration of nullity on the strength of a certification by the Civil Registrar of Manila that, after a diligent and exhaustive search, the alleged marriage license indicated in the marriage certificate does not appear in the records and cannot be found. Decide the case and explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: The petition should be granted. The marriage of Brad and Angelina is void ab initio.
- Legal basis: A valid marriage license is a formal requisite of marriage (Art. 3 (2), Family Code). The absence of any formal requisite renders the marriage void ab initio (Art. 4). A civil registrar's certification of due search and inability to find the license can prove its absence (Republic v. Court of Appeals and Castro, G.R. No. 103047, September 2, 1994).
- Application: The marriage was celebrated in 1995, so the Family Code governs. The Civil Registrar of Manila, the official custodian of license records, certified after a diligent and exhaustive search that the license does not exist. This shows a total absence of the license, not a mere irregularity. No facts show an exemption from the license requirement, such as five years of cohabitation without legal impediment (Art. 34).
- Conclusion: The court should declare the marriage void ab initio for lack of a marriage license.

# Objective and multiple-choice quiz (Year 1-2)
Q (multiple choice): Which of these is an ESSENTIAL requisite of marriage under Art. 2 of the Family Code?
A) A valid marriage license
B) Authority of the solemnizing officer
C) Consent freely given in the presence of the solemnizing officer
D) A marriage ceremony with two witnesses
Correct: C
Hint: Art. 2 lists only two essential requisites; the others in this list are in Art. 3.
Hint: One essential requisite is legal capacity; the other is about the parties' will.
Why: Art. 2 lists legal capacity of the contracting parties and consent freely given in the presence of the solemnizing officer.

Q (multiple choice): Under Art. 4 of the Family Code, what is the effect of a DEFECT in an essential requisite?
A) The marriage is void ab initio
B) The marriage is voidable
C) The marriage is valid, but the parties are liable
D) The marriage is unenforceable
Correct: B
Hint: Art. 4 treats the absence of a requisite differently from a defect in one.
Hint: The answer refers you to Art. 45 (annulment).
Why: Under Art. 4, a defect in any essential requisite makes the marriage voidable as provided in Art. 45.

Q (objective): Under Art. 4, the absence of any essential or formal requisite makes the marriage void ______. (Two Latin words.)
Accept: ab initio | void ab initio
Hint: It means "from the beginning".
Why: Art. 4 says the absence of any essential or formal requisite renders the marriage void ab initio, except as stated in Art. 35(2).

Q (objective): Under Art. 34, a man and a woman need no marriage license if they have lived together as husband and wife for at least how many years without any legal impediment? (Give the number.)
Accept: 5 | five | five years | 5 years
Hint: It is more than three and fewer than ten.
Why: Art. 34 exempts from the license requirement a couple who lived together as husband and wife for at least five years without any legal impediment to marry each other.
